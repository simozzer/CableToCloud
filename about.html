Learn networking by building it

CableToCloud is a free game that runs in your browser. You plug in cables, give devices addresses, set up routers, and watch packets travel. When something doesn’t work, it tells you why in plain English.

Mistakes explain themselves

Real networks fail silently: a ping just times out. In CableToCloud the simulation knows exactly where the packet stopped, and says so. Learning to read those clues is learning to troubleshoot.

Sales-PC> open \\hr.office Opening \\hr.office ... Windows can't find \\hr.office. Why? Sales-PC couldn't get an answer from its DNS server 10.1.99.53. Router1's access rules blocked UDP port 53 from 10.1.10.100 to 10.1.99.53. No rule matched, so the built-in “deny everything else” at the end of the list blocked it. Every name has to be looked up in DNS before anything else happens. Permit DNS (UDP port 53 to 10.1.99.53), or end the list with permit any → any.

What you’ll learn

Each level adds one new idea, with a short briefing, hints if you need them, and a model solution at the end. Your answer doesn’t have to match it: any network that works is accepted, and the game points out anything that could be tidier.

Who it’s for

Curious beginners

No experience needed. Level 1 starts with plugging a single computer into a home router.

People refreshing old knowledge

Learned this years ago? The levels move quickly from basics to DMZs, VLANs and jump hosts.

Students

Covers the IPv4 fundamentals in most introductory networking courses and entry-level certifications, with practice that a textbook can’t give.

Teachers

Nothing to install and no accounts: share a link, or open index.html straight from a folder. Teacher mode (in the Levels menu) unlocks every level.

How levels are checked

  • Live objectives. The checklist ticks itself as your network starts working.
  • Nothing counts until it all works at once. If a later change breaks a step you had already done, that step turns red.
  • A review when you finish. Unused firewall rules, oversized subnets or a static address inside a DHCP pool are pointed out, even when everything works.
  • Hints, then the answer. Hints give the solution away one step at a time; the model solution is always one click away.

What’s simplified

The simulation follows how real networks behave, but leaves some things out to keep the focus on the ideas:

  • IPv4 only. No IPv6, and no Wi-Fi radio: every connection is a cable.
  • Routers know their own networks plus a default route. There are no routing protocols (such as OSPF) or extra static routes.
  • Switches just work. They learn instantly; there are no MAC address tables or loop prevention to manage.
  • Connections are a request and a reply. TCP handshakes, timeouts and packet sizes aren’t modelled.
  • A small Internet. A few public servers (such as 8.8.8.8 and www.example.com) and one customer.
  • Firewall rules are simple. Replies to allowed connections are let back automatically, as a stateful firewall would.

Behind the scenes

CableToCloud is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no dependencies, and the source is on GitHub. The simulation and every level are covered by an automated test suite, including checks that each level’s model solution works and that plausible wrong answers don’t.
